# Political Retrospective

A submodule of **Retrospective Intelligence** in **PRP** (Personal Resource Planner, by JDP-Iq): https://hp-retrospective.pages.dev (also inside PRP at https://jdpi.pages.dev/#/rs/retrospective). It uses the shared PRP stylesheet and connector (`https://jdpi.pages.dev/design/prp.css`, `prp-bar.js`), light by default with a dark theme.

Move the slider from **1926 to the present**:
- **The map redraws each state's borders** for that year, from Cliopatria's rows for the year (borders are by the year, in Cliopatria's sample years; 2025 and 2026 are drawn on 2024's, the last year Cliopatria holds). Each country is coloured by what happened that year: new leader, irregular seizure of power, Cliopatria's border differs from the year before's, or no change.
- **Clicking a country** shows who was in charge that year, how they took and left power, other office-holders, and that year's key events (elections, referendums, leadership changes, and years in which Cliopatria's border differs from the year before's). Each one links to Wikipedia, plus a "Year in [country]" page where Wikipedia has one.
- **Borders that differ:** a border event is a year in which a state's area in Cliopatria differs from the year before's by more than 1%, which may be a redrawing of Cliopatria's rows rather than a change of border, so the page says that Cliopatria's border differs, never that the border changed. Over a period of years, **Play … borders** steps through the period's first year and each year its border differs, and **Download GIF** saves those frames as an animated GIF.
- **Not drawn:** the covered states VM cannot place on a free border for the year the map draws are named under the map ("VM cannot place … on a free border for 1950"), whether or not a state is selected. Cliopatria gives one border a year, so a change within a year cannot be shown or animated; a single year says so.
- **With no country selected,** the panel lists every key event that year across all covered countries.
- **Map and Table** are tabs: the table lists every covered state on the map in the selected year, who was in charge and what happened, and sorts by any column. `?view=table` links straight to it; the `#y=…&c=…` hash still sets the year and country.
- **Addresses:** `c` is the state's Wikidata item (`#y=1962&c=Q30`). An address from before 20 November 2026 with a Gleditsch and Ward number (`#y=1962&c=2`) opens the same state and is rewritten to its item, through `oldKeys` in `data/countries.json`.
- **Search** finds countries, leaders, and Cliopatria's own names for the rows that stand for a covered state (Weimar Republic, Nazi Germany). Picking a leader jumps to their first year in office.

Coverage: the same 78 countries as Political Atlas, plus predecessor states (Weimar and Nazi Germany, West and East Germany, the Soviet Union, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, the Republic of China), keyed by Wikidata item (`scripts/countries.mjs`). A state is covered while it is an independent state in Wikidata: its statehood (`statehood` in `data/countries.json`, built by `scripts/build-statehood.mjs` from each state's inception and dissolution statements and those of the items before and after it) gives one span for each spell, from 1926 or the day it began to the day it ended, and a change of constitution is never a break. Until 21 November 2026 a state was covered from its first leader to its last, so Ukraine was "covered" from July 1990; now, outside its statehood, a state's panel says "Not an independent state at the end of <year>", and where a state exists but VM holds no leader for the date (the Netherlands from May 1940 to June 1945), its panel says "VM holds no leader for <date>". Cliopatria's polity rows are matched to the states by name and year in `scripts/cliopatria-states.mjs`; rows that stand for no covered state appear uncoloured.

## Rebuild the data

Borders (when Cliopatria or the crosswalk changes):

```bash
npm run build-borders -- --zip <path to cliopatria.geojson.zip>
```

The archive is `cliopatria.geojson.zip` from the v0.2.1 tag (https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria/tree/v0.2.1), kept outside the repository; the script checks its sha256 and stops if it is another file. It writes `data/borders.topo.json` (about 620 KB, 150 KB gzipped) and rewrites the border parts of `data/countries.json` (spans, border events), printing each state's years with no row ("VM cannot place it on a free border").

Leaders, events and parties:

```bash
npm run build-data
```

You need `raw/Archigos_4.1_stata14.dta` and the borders file above; Wikidata's election results are cached in `build/`; the statehood and leader steps ask Wikidata and Wikipedia afresh on every run (throttled, and stopping on a second "too many requests" answer), so a rebuild takes whatever Wikidata holds that day. The script writes `data/countries.json` in its second shape ('retro-countries/2', `scripts/retro-shape.mjs`).

jdpi reads `data/countries.json` from this repository's main branch in three scheduled workflows (calendar-refresh.yml, lake-refresh.yml, national-refresh.yml); a change to the file's shape is a change to what they read.

## Test

```bash
npm test
```

This runs end-to-end tests in your installed Edge. Set `RETRO_URL` to test the live site.

## Known limits

- Borders are by the year, in Cliopatria's sample years, so a change within a year (the Anschluss in March 1938, say) cannot be dated or animated; the border animation and GIF of before 20 November 2026, which moved day by day, now move year by year. 2025 and 2026 are drawn on 2024's borders.
- A covered state with no row of its own in Cliopatria for a year is named on the page as one "VM cannot place on a free border" for that year: Liechtenstein and San Marino in every year; Canada 1926–31; Haiti 1926–35; the states Cliopatria draws inside the Axis powers' lands in the Second World War (Albania 1939–43, Poland 1940–52, the Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg and Monaco 1940–44, Iceland 1940–45, Denmark 1941–44, Greece and Yugoslavia 1941–44, Italy 1944); Japan 1946–52; El Salvador 1979–91; Czechoslovakia 1939–46, 1969–90 and 1992; most of the Soviet and Yugoslav successors in 1991, the year they became states, and Latvia in 1990–91; and new states in their first years (Cyprus 1960, Jamaica and Trinidad and Tobago 1962, Malta 1964–66, Barbados 1966, Grenada 1974–75, Dominica 1978, Belize 1981–82). Since 21 November 2026 a state is covered only inside its statehood (Wikidata), so a colony is no longer named before independence (the Bahamas 1967–72, Belize 1961–80), nor Ukraine in 1990. `npm run build-borders` prints the full list (41 of the 79 states have at least one such year).
- Cliopatria's rows hold lands a state ruled but that were not part of it, so a state's area can be far larger than the state: Greenland with Denmark from 1946, Algeria with France from 1958 to 2023, Portugal's and Japan's empires, Nazi Germany's occupied lands, fragments far away (the United States row of 1967–2005 holds pieces in Indochina, Micronesia and Berlin). A border change is any year whose area differs from the year before's by more than 1%, so some are Cliopatria's redrawings (Luxembourg's rows differ by 2–6% in 1930, 1932, 1936 and 1958), and the page says "Cliopatria's border differs", not that the border moved.
- The statehood build reads the start time (P580) of Wikidata's "instance of: sovereign state" statement, not its end time (P582): a state ends by its dissolution date (P576) or by a pick listed in `scripts/build-statehood.mjs`. On 21 November 2026 five of the states' items carried such an end time (the Soviet Union, the Kingdom of Yugoslavia, Czechoslovakia, Nazi Germany and West Germany), and each is already ended or carried on by its dissolution date or its listed pick. A state whose only recorded end is that end time would be read as still existing (second review, X3).
- Under "Also in office", people who took office on the same day are listed head of state first and then by name, and the list is the same from one build to the next. Where Wikidata reports one term (the same person, office and start) with two different ends, the build keeps one: an end given to the day before one given only to the year, then the later. On 21 November 2026 three terms had two ends: Mark Rutte's (2 July 2024 and "2024"), Cemal Gürsel's (2 February and 28 March 1966) and Turgut Özal's (16 and 17 April 1993); the page shows 2 July 2024, 28 March 1966 and 17 April 1993. Three more picks follow a fixed rule and not the order of Wikidata's answer: votes held on one day are listed by title; a leader whose two offices carry the same dates in Wikidata is named by the office whose item has the lower number (on 21 November 2026: Saud, Faisal and Khalid of Saudi Arabia as King and not Prime Minister, Svetozar Marović as President of Serbia and Montenegro, and Masayoshi Ito as Prime Minister of Japan, whom Wikidata also has as Acting Prime Minister for the same days); and where a statement of dissolution carries two causes, the event's text names the first by its label. Party memberships of equal rank keep the order of the person's Wikidata item.
- Statehood events (independence, union or end of a state) come from Wikidata since 21 November 2026 (until 20 November they came from CShapes, and for a day there were none). Each date is shown at Wikidata's precision: Austria's return as a state in 1955 is given to the year only, so its span starts on 1 January 1955 and its event reads "1955". Wikidata links no item for interwar Estonia to the Estonian SSR, so Estonia's end in 1940 is the start of the Estonian SSR (21 July 1940). Most dates differ from CShapes' (and Gleditsch and Ward's) by days or weeks; the larger differences are of definition: CShapes starts West and East Germany in 1945 as occupied units and draws Austria without a break from 1919 to 1955, and it starts most of the Soviet and Yugoslav successors on the day it ends the Soviet Union or Yugoslavia (26 December 1991, 27 April 1992) and the Baltic states' return on 6 September 1991, where Wikidata mostly takes the declarations (Ukraine 24 August 1991, Lithuania 11 March 1990). The Baltic and Georgian dates are Wikidata's own items' dates, and differ in kind: Lithuania is counted again from its declaration of 11 March 1990, Latvia from 4 May 1990 and Estonia from 20 August 1991, the days Wikidata ends the Latvian and Estonian Soviet republics, and Georgia from 25 December 1991, its inception as a sovereign state in Wikidata; in 1940 Latvia ends on 17 June, Estonia and Lithuania on 21 July. So at the end of 1990 Latvia and Lithuania are counted and Estonia is not. Austria from 1945 to 1955 is counted as not an independent state, as Germany is from 1945 to 1949, and unlike Japan from 1945 to 1952: Wikidata ends the item for Allied-occupied Austria in 1955, to the year only, and VM follows it, though Austria's own item gives no break after 1945; this is a choice, left for VM's owner to confirm.
- A leader list can start before a state's statehood or run past its end (Archigos starts Ukraine's Kravchuk on 18 July 1990, before its independence on 24 August 1991). Those leaders, and the events dated outside the statehood, are listed apart in the state's panel, under "While not an independent state", and are left out of the overview's leaders, the year's counts, the colours and the year's list of key events.
- Leaders' and office holders' dates are kept at Wikidata's precision: a date Wikidata gives only to the year or the month carries `startPrecision` or `endPrecision` ("year", "month") in `data/countries.json`, and the page shows it as "2020" or "Jan 2020", never as 1 January; a leader's arrival given only to the year carries `precision` on its event. Where Wikidata holds both a year-only and a day statement for the same person and office in the same year, the day statement is kept.
- A leader matched to Wikidata is labelled by the office held at the end of the run matched (`position`); a run of terms in more than one office (Ilham Aliyev, Prime Minister then President of Azerbaijan, 2003) keeps each term in `positions`. A run is joined across offices only where that brings it nearer Archigos's dates. An office whose Wikidata label does not read as an office (by its title or the state's name) is labelled from its English Wikipedia article or its multilingual label, or left without a label, and listed in `offices-doubtful.tsv` in the report folder.
- Archigos gives some leaders by surname only. Where Wikidata has no match, the leader links to a Wikipedia search instead of a specific article.
- After 2015, each country follows the same office Archigos tracked (head of state or head of government), so a strong leader in the other office appears under "Also in office". Example: Serbia's President Vučić since 2017.
- Where Archigos and Wikidata leave a leader list short of the present (Guatemala, Poland, Slovakia, Moldova, Romania, Israel, Saudi Arabia, Jamaica, Switzerland and Greece in November 2026), `scripts/leader-corrections.mjs` completes it, each entry checked against Wikidata and Wikipedia; `npm run build-data` applies it, and `node scripts/leader-corrections.mjs` patches `data/countries.json` on its own.
- Archigos has no record of Montenegro after 1918 or of the ten microstates (Dominica, Grenada, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Antigua and Barbuda, Saint Kitts and Nevis, Monaco, Liechtenstein, Andorra, San Marino), so their lists are built by hand in `scripts/leader-lists.mjs` (RT-04, November 2026): each entry read from at least two of Wikidata, Wikipedia's lists and rulers.org, with its Wikidata id, from the state's first day (independence; 1926 for the four European microstates; Montenegro from its independence on 3 June 2006, nine days before its first day on the map, as Archigos starts Kosovo, Czechia and Slovakia) to the present. `node scripts/leader-corrections.mjs` then `node scripts/build-parties.mjs <their Wikidata keys, or their old GW codes>` applies them to `data/countries.json`. The choices made there:
  - Cliopatria draws eight of the ten microstates (Monaco, Andorra and the six Caribbean states); Liechtenstein and San Marino have no row, so the map never draws them; their panel opens from search or an address such as `#y=2020&c=Q238`, and the table (states on the map) leaves them out.
  - The six Caribbean states and Montenegro follow the Prime Minister (the President, or the British monarch, is under "Also in office"). A Caribbean list starts on independence day, when the office of Prime Minister began; the same person's earlier years as Premier are not included. Montenegro's first entries (Đukanović, and Vujanović as President) start at independence, where Wikipedia's list splits Đukanović's term of 8 January 2003, so nothing from its years in Serbia and Montenegro shows.
  - Grenada from 1979 to 1984 follows those who held power: Bishop's People's Revolutionary Government (coup of 13 March 1979), Coard (from 14 October 1983), Austin's Revolutionary Military Council (from 19 October, removed by the US-led invasion), the Governor-General Paul Scoon as interim head of government, then Brathwaite's Interim Advisory Council, as Wikipedia's list does.
  - Monaco follows the Sovereign Prince, who holds executive power; Albert II is dated from his accession on 6 April 2005, not his regency from 31 March. The Minister of State is under "Also in office", with no party: an appointed official (Wikidata calls most of them "independent politician", which the page would list as a party in power). Two changes of Minister follow the Journal de Monaco's ordinances (15 January 1932, 31 May 1937); five others keep rulers.org's days where French Wikipedia differs by days and no official text was read.
  - Liechtenstein follows the Prince, or the member of his house who exercises his powers: Franz Joseph as regent from 30 March 1938 (Liechtenstein's national historical lexicon and rulers.org; his English Wikipedia infobox says 31 March), Hans-Adam as his father's deputy from 26 August 1984, Hereditary Prince Alois since 15 August 2004 (as National Analysis names him). The reigning Prince and the Head of Government are under "Also in office"; in 1928 the lexicon's days are followed there too (Schädler's government resigned on 15 June, Prince Alfred ran it from 24 June, so the days between are empty).
  - Andorra follows the General Syndic, who led the Valleys' government, until the office of Head of Government was created in January 1982 (the four days from 4 to 8 January 1982 are left empty), then the Head of Government. From 19 August 1933, when French gendarmes had dismissed the General Council and Pallarès left for exile, to the next Syndic's election on 18 September the office is left empty too (rulers.org). Two Syndics are named as the General Council's own index of office holders and rulers.org name them (Pere Font Altimir, Francesc Molné Armengol), not as Wikidata's labels do. Its two Co-Princes, the French head of state and the Bishop of Urgell, are under "Also in office"; the French Co-Prince carries no party, and the Bishop's office is empty from Iglesias's resignation on 29 April 1969 to Malla's start on 19 June. Boris Skossyreff, who proclaimed himself king in July 1934, never held power and is not listed.
  - San Marino follows its two Captains Regent, who preside over the collective executive and change on 1 April and 1 October: Switzerland's pattern (the chair of a collective executive), with both entered for each semester, so its overview joins them with "and" and each semester adds two leader changes to a year's count. In 1930 one died on 10 September (the sources do not say how, so his exit is unknown) and his successor took office on 16 September, which the overview gives as "until" and "joined by … from"; from 11 to 27 October 1957 the provisional government of four formed at Rovereta held power. San Marino has no other head of state or government, so nobody is under "Also in office" (Political Atlas names the Secretary of State for Foreign and Political Affairs for its Congress of State; that office was not added). Two Captains Regent (1930, 1938) have no Wikidata item and say so in the data.
  - Dominica, June 1979: Patrick John is dated to 21 June (Wikidata, his own article, rulers.org) and Oliver Seraphin from 25 June (Wikidata, his own article, Wikipedia's list), each date as three of the four sources give it, so the four days between are left empty. The two lists hand over on one day each, Wikipedia's on 25 June and rulers.org's on 21 June, the day Jenner Armour was sworn in as acting President.
  - Saint Lucia, 2007: Stephenson King is dated from 1 May 2007, when strokes disabled John Compton and King became acting Prime Minister (Wikidata, rulers.org, King's article; Wikipedia's list dates him from Compton's death on 7 September), as Archigos dates Ehud Olmert from Ariel Sharon's stroke, and Compton's exit is Sharon's, "Retired Due to Ill Health". Compton briefly resumed power in early June 2007 to reshuffle the cabinet; that is not a separate entry.
  - Grenada: Nicholas Brathwaite's non-party Interim Advisory Council of 1983-84 carries no party (Wikidata would give the National Democratic Congress, founded in 1987).
  - Entry and exit manner is given only where a source says it (Grenada's coups and invasion, deaths in office); otherwise it is unknown, as the page shows it.
- Election coverage depends on Wikidata. A few elections that Wikidata doesn't link to the country are missing, for example Germany's March 1933 election.
- Where the Wikidata election fetch gives a wrong date or files an election under the wrong country, `scripts/election-corrections.mjs` corrects it, each date read in at least two places and named there; `node scripts/leader-corrections.mjs` (and `npm run build-data`) applies it. Since 17 November 2026: Saint Vincent and the Grenadines' 2025 election was held on 27 November 2025 (Wikipedia's article and St Vincent Times; Wikidata still gives 30 November, a forecast referenced to a 2020 IPU page); Grenada's 2026 election on 5 November 2026 (Wikidata, Wikipedia, news reports; the cached fetch held the due date, 30 June 2027); Grenada's 1967 and 2003 elections, which Wikidata gives Spain as country, are under Grenada only (24 August 1967 and 27 November 2003, Wikidata and Wikipedia). Since 21 November 2026 a corrected election that falls outside its state's statehood is not listed: Grenada's 1967 election came before its independence in 1974, so only the 2003 one is.
